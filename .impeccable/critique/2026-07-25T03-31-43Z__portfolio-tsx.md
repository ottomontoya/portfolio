## Design Specificity Verdict

**One room out of five is authored for this product. The other four are a well-dressed template.**

**LLM assessment.** The design *world* is specific and has teeth: five named walls, one accent under 10% of any viewport, hairlines instead of cards, exactly one oxblood italic per headline, pill-or-line at small scale. DESIGN.md's refusal to imitate Tableau chrome is a real position, not a mood board. And it works — nothing here reads as AI-generated portfolio slop.

Unmistakably this product's: **the project row** (`portfolio.css:270`, `components/Sections.tsx:79`) — a `64px 1.4fr 1fr 32px` grid carrying index, argument and metric on one hairline-separated line. That is a table row, and a table row is the analyst's native object. Its hover gesture is the best interaction on the site: the row gains 24px of padding and a warm-paper fill, so it *widens into the page* rather than lifting off it — spatially consistent with a flat paper world where only glass floats. Also specific: the `X → Y` migration idiom, the `scope / timeline / stack` consulting triad, and the section-aware nav re-scheming, which drives two IntersectionObservers with *opposite* rootMargins (`Nav.tsx:50`, `Nav.tsx:58`) so each pill is tested against the room actually beneath it.

Template-interchangeable: the four SVG room dividers (`components/Divider.tsx`) — 2010s section-shape decoration; a sine wave between Skills and Experience says nothing about either. The hero composition — eyebrow → oversized serif → lead → two pills → four-stat strip is the canonical 2024 portfolio arrangement, differentiated only by palette. The Skills room — a résumé skills block in a nicer color, and it is the loudest wall on the site. The `200px 1fr` timeline.

And the sharpest thing to say about this site: **the sparklines are fabricated.** `Charts.tsx:39` generates scatter points from `Math.sin(i * 13.37)`; the rest are hardcoded arrays; `bars` is reused verbatim by projects 02 and 06 (`projects.ts:58`, `:151`); the mark bears no relation to the project it labels — why is 04, "workflows automated," an *up*-trending area? DESIGN.md defends them as texture. But on the portfolio of someone whose stated method is "I validate every metric against source systems before a dashboard goes to production" (`data/about.ts:16`), decorative invented data is the one aesthetic move that actively refutes the positioning.

That is why the Skills room can hold 31 undifferentiated items and still be "on system," and why a site claiming cognitive-UI implementation craft never shows a single information-design decision.

## Overall Impression

This is a good site with two holes in it, and both holes are in load-bearing walls.

The good part is real: the room system is genuine wayfinding, the project row is the right object for this candidate, and the content is typed data with a coercive interface (`projects.ts:3`) that makes a project *physically unable* to ship without a scope, a metric, and an outcome. That is PRODUCT.md's evidence principle enforced by the compiler. Most portfolios keep this in JSX and drift within three edits.

The two holes: **dark mode is not a theme, it is a hazard** — two of five rooms and the primary CTA fall below 3:1 — and **the six most important interactions on the site are unreachable by keyboard and invisible to screen readers.** Both land directly on PRODUCT.md's own commitments: the accessibility baseline says the toggle "must keep contrast intact in both themes" (it does not), and Principle 5 says "sloppy execution refutes the positioning" (`<li onClick>` on a site claiming cognitive-UI craft does exactly that).

The single biggest opportunity is not aesthetic. It is that the site's emotional peak — the overlay, where "696 → 12" sits at 48px beside three paragraphs of genuine reasoning — is walled off from the conversion. The visitor who has just been fully convinced can only close a 36px `×`. Everything needed for a strong close already exists in the files; it is ordered wrong.

## What's Working

**1. The room system is real wayfinding, implemented against the live DOM.** Five full-bleed walls mean the visitor always knows where they are without reading a heading, and the glass pill re-schemes to stay legible over each one. It works because the classification is driven by two IntersectionObservers with *opposite* rootMargins — `0px 0px -(vh-80)px` for the top-anchored desktop pill, `-(vh-80)px 0px 0px` for the bottom-anchored mobile pill (`Nav.tsx:50`, `:58`) — so each pill is tested against the room beneath *it*, not a shared threshold, and both rebuild on resize. That is why the most fragile element on the page — translucency over five different colors — never becomes illegible in light mode.

**2. The project row is the right object for this candidate.** A data table rendered at editorial scale. It lets the visitor scan one axis at a time — all six indices, or all six titles, or all six metrics — which is how a comparison read actually happens, with no card chrome to parse. The hover response is spatial (padding + fill) rather than decorative (shadow + lift), which keeps the flat-paper world intact. It is the one place where the aesthetic and the positioning do the same job.

**3. Content-as-data with a coercive type.** The `Project` interface requires `scope`, `timeline`, `metric`, `impact`, and `responsibilities` on every entry (`projects.ts:3`), with `caseStudy`/`images` optional and handled without breaking layout. The type is the enforcement mechanism behind "evidence over adjectives" — a project cannot ship without an outcome attached.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Six of the site's most important interactions are unreachable by keyboard and invisible to screen readers

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md Principle 3 puts the reasoning — the actual argument for hiring — inside the overlay. For any keyboard or screen-reader user that argument does not exist, and it isn't merely hard to reach: a screen reader announces "list, 6 items" of static text with no indication that more content exists. Beyond the ethics, a portfolio claiming cognitive-UI implementation craft that ships `<li onClick>` refutes its own positioning in the one artifact a hiring manager can actually inspect.

**Evidence.** `Sections.tsx:79` — `<li className="project" onClick={...}>` with no `role`, `tabIndex`, or key handler. `ProjectOverlay.tsx:27-29` — no `role="dialog"`, no `aria-modal`, no `aria-labelledby`, no focus move on open, no focus trap, no focus return on close. `ProjectOverlay.tsx:33` — the lightbox opens from `<img onClick>`, unreachable by keyboard, no visible close. Zero `:focus`/`:focus-visible`/`outline` declarations in 447 lines of CSS (detector-verified) — UA rings survive only because nothing sets `outline: none`, and they were never designed against five wall colors. Tab order: brand → 5 nav links → toggle → 2 hero CTAs → *nothing until* the LinkedIn link and the `mailto:`.

**Fix.** Make the row a real control: wrap its content in `<button class="project-btn">` (or `role="button" tabIndex={0}` + `onKeyDown` for Enter/Space) at `Sections.tsx:79`, plus `aria-haspopup="dialog"`. On the overlay card: `role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-labelledby`, focus on mount, trap Tab, store the trigger and `.focus()` it on close. Author the focus ring once — a 2px `currentColor` outline at 2px offset on `:focus-visible` re-colors itself per room automatically, the same trick `.cta-ghost` already uses. Add `aria-current="page"` to the active nav link, `aria-pressed` to the theme toggle, accessible names to the five `<section>`s, and a skip link. Promote `.tl-role` and `.edu-title` from `<div>` to real headings — "Data Analyst at STX Next" and "MIT Schwarzman College of Computing" are currently unreachable by heading navigation.

### [P0] Contrast fails in both themes, and dark mode structurally breaks two of five rooms

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md sets exactly one accessibility commitment: "the light/dark toggle must keep contrast intact in both themes." It does not. The failure list includes the primary CTA's own label and the hero's headline figures — the two elements the entire persuade goal rests on.

**Root cause.** DESIGN.md's Semantic Swap Rule is elegant on paper and unsound in arithmetic: it assumes every wall is `--beige` or `--ink`, but two of five are `--green`/`--burgundy`. `.room-about` and `.room-skills` set `color: var(--beige)` (`portfolio.css:164`, `:166`), which flips to near-black `#1a1a18` in dark mode while the wall only *brightens* to `#3d6b3f`/`#a8344a`. Both move the wrong way at once.

**Dark-mode failures (computed):**

| Pair | Where | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| `#1a1a18` on `#a8344a` — **primary CTA label, both CTAs** | `portfolio.css:215` | **2.70:1** |
| `#a8344a` on `#1a1a18` — hero display italic + all four stat values | `:189`, `:232` | **2.70:1** |
| Entire About room body text | `:164`, `:243` | **2.55:1** |
| Entire Skills room | `:166` | **2.70:1** |
| Project index numbers (oxblood @70%) | `:278` | **1.90:1** |
| `.overlay-side` `#232220` against `.overlay-card` `#1a1a18` | `:406` | **1.08:1** — the tonal step DESIGN.md relies on ceases to exist |

Plus the hairlines invert wrongly, because they are hardcoded rgba rather than tokens. In dark mode the Work room becomes `#1a1a18`, so all six `rgba(0,0,0,.15)` row separators vanish (`:271`, `:274`) — the system's *only* structural device, gone from its signature component. Symmetrically, the Experience room becomes bone paper, so every `rgba(255,255,255,.12–.15)` rule vanishes: timeline, contact block, contact rows, footer.

**Light-mode failures (computed):** the nav active indicator, `#fff` on `rgba(0,0,0,.32)` glass, is **~2.6:1** at 13px — and that token is *committed* in DESIGN.md, so the spec itself needs revising; white on 32% black cannot be made to pass over bone. The `filter: brightness()` accent trick gives **2.22:1** on the Experience headline italic (`:199`) and **2.47:1** on the contact headline italic "together." (`:344`) — both below the 3:1 large-text floor, on the site's closing line. And every mono label at ≤60% opacity fails 4.5:1 on every room except charcoal: ink @50% on bone **3.20:1** (`:417`), @55% **3.70:1** (`:289`, `:396`, `:235`), @60% **4.30:1** (`:283`, `:387`); bone @60% on field green **4.12:1** and on oxblood **4.36:1** (`:252`). DESIGN.md's Label token specifies "55–70% opacity" — as specified, the bottom half of that range is unshippable on every wall in both themes.

For the record, the full-strength pairs are excellent: oxblood on bone 9.4:1, bone on green 8.2:1, bone on oxblood 9.4:1, bone on ink 14.5:1. **The palette is not the problem.** The opacity floor and the swap logic are.

**Fix.** (a) Introduce `--on-wall` tokens and set each room's text from the token matching its *actual rendered* wall per theme, so About/Skills keep light text in dark mode. (b) Replace every hardcoded hairline with one `--rule` variable per theme, or `color-mix(in srgb, currentColor 15%, transparent)`, which inverts correctly for free. (c) Retire the `filter: brightness()` trick; define a real `--accent-on-dark` around `#c1546a` that clears 4.5:1 on charcoal. (d) Raise the label-opacity floor to ~72%. (e) Add `prefers-color-scheme` initialization and persistence — the toggle currently ignores the OS preference and forgets on reload (`portfolio.tsx:11`, `utils/theme.ts`).

**(f) Amend DESIGN.md in the same pass — user-approved, in scope, not optional.** Two committed tokens cannot pass as specified, so the design record must change with the code or the next build re-introduces the failures:

- **`nav-link-active`** (DESIGN.md frontmatter): `backgroundColor: rgba(0,0,0,.32)` + `textColor: #ffffff` computes to ~2.6:1 at 13px over bone. White on 32% black cannot be made to pass over a light wall — replace the token, do not tune it.
- **Label typography token**: "55–70% opacity" — the bottom half of that range fails 4.5:1 on every wall except charcoal, in both themes. Restate the floor at ~72%.
- Also update the **Colors → Named Rules → The Semantic Swap Rule** prose, whose stated assumption (every wall is `--beige` or `--ink`) is the root cause of the dark-mode breakage, and the **Elevation & Depth** entry if the `--rule` hairline variable changes the documented 10–18% alpha range.

Decision provenance: the user explicitly approved folding the DESIGN.md amendment into this step when the critique's action plan was presented (2026-07-24 session). This is an authorized design-record change, not incidental drift repair.

- `.side-h { border-bottom: 1px solid currentColor }` at element `opacity: .6` (`portfolio.css:252`) yields a ~60%-alpha rule where DESIGN.md specifies 10–18% — four instances, each four times heavier than every other line on the page, both in the loudest rooms.
- `theme-color` is `#2d4a32` (`index.html:15`, repeated in PRODUCT.md) but field green is `#2d4a2b`. The mobile browser chrome is a color that exists nowhere in the palette.
- `.proj-n` carries two competing `font-size` declarations of equal specificity — `13px` (`:278`) and `.mono`'s `.85em` (`:181`) — resolved only by source order, so the real size is ~13.6px. `.proj-meta` and `.proj-ml` collide the same way.
- `framer-motion@^11` is declared and imported nowhere (detector-verified). Remove the dependency or the PRODUCT.md claim.
- Two orphaned TTFs ship in `public/fonts/` from an earlier direction — PeaceSans 150 KB, OpenSauceSans 61 KB — referenced by nothing.
- The Google Fonts request loads DM Sans at weight 700 (`index.html:36`), which DESIGN.md's No-Bold rule forbids and the CSS never uses. Render-blocking, third-party, no `preload`.
- `.foot` says "Made with care" — the only unearned adjective on the site, in the last position anyone reads, on a page whose first principle is evidence over adjectives.
- The LinkedIn `aria-label` sits on the inner `<svg>`, not the `<a>` (`Sections.tsx:178`), so the link's accessible name depends on SVG name computation.
- `.nav-brand` is a `<button>` that scrolls, so cmd-click, middle-click and "open in new tab" all do nothing on the element every visitor treats as "home."
- Hero stats are three sibling `<div>`s; the value→label→suffix relationship is purely visual. A `<dl>` would survive a screen reader.
- Education sits in the Skills room while Experience gets its own wall — a recruiter looking for a degree looks under Experience. None of the three entries is a certification, though the heading says "Education & certifications."
- No `color-scheme` declaration, so the custom scrollbar thumb `rgba(0,0,0,.18)` is invisible on the dark page in dark mode.
- `ProjectOverlay.tsx:18`'s body-overflow lock quietly fights `#root { overflow-x: hidden }` — the same interaction that produced the last two commits on this branch.
- `.work-head` is a flex row with a 48px gap holding an 18ch headline and a 36ch lead; between 860px and ~1100px these will crowd. *Needs visual confirmation.*
- Reverse-chronological `<ol>` for the timeline numbers newest→oldest semantically; harmless while the numbers are hidden.

## Questions to Consider

1. **If the sparklines plotted the actual numbers, would you need any other proof that you can design information?** The one place on the page where you could *demonstrate* craft instead of claiming it currently holds `Math.sin(i * 13.37)`.
2. **Does the project detail need to be a modal at all, or is it a sixth room?** Making the rows links to `#p1`…`#p6` fixes keyboard access, focus return, shareability, back-button behavior and crawler parity in one move — and puts the reasoning on the same footing as the rest of the page instead of behind a scrim with no exit.
3. **Where on this page does a visitor see a design decision you made?** Anonymity forbids client screenshots. It does not forbid a diagram of your own access model, your own five-folder taxonomy, your own KPI tree, or a before/after of an information structure. Those are your artifacts, not the client's — and their absence is the largest unexploited opportunity in the brief.
4. **If dark mode were deleted tomorrow, what would the site lose?** It currently costs a broken theme in eight places and occupies the nav's most valuable slot — where a contact link could live — in exchange for a preference the site does not even read from the OS.
5. **Six projects, all "selected." What does the ordering mean?** An unexplained 01–06 on an analyst's site invites the reader to assume rank, then be confused when 06 (3 weeks, implementation) outranks 04 (ongoing, end-to-end).
6. **What is the 90-second version of this page?** If you had to prove scale, range and ownership in one viewport with no scroll, what three things would be on it — and why aren't those three things the hero?
